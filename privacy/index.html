Privacy Policy for Pocket Pressure

Last updated: 2026-10-20

Pocket Pressure is published by Little Lost Projects. Contact details are at the end of this policy.

The short version

Pocket Pressure has no server, no account of its own, no analytics and no ads, and we (Little Lost Projects) never receive your readings, notes, profiles or settings. Everything you enter is stored on your device, encrypted, until you delete it or uninstall the app. There are three things you can choose to do that put a copy outside the app's own storage: turn on Health Connect export, which copies your readings into Android's own on-device health store; export or share a CSV or PDF file; and, with Premium, turn on sync across devices, which keeps a copy in a private, app-only folder in your own Google Drive so your other phones signed in to the same Google account can see your readings (see "Sync across devices" below). All three are off or manual unless you use them.

The app offers an optional one-time "Premium" purchase through Google Play. That purchase, and the check of whether you own it, is handled by Google Play, not by us, and, together with the optional sync feature, it is the reason the app has internet permission (see "Google Play purchases" and "Sync across devices" below). In Google Play's Data safety section this is declared as purchase history, collected for app functionality and not shared. Your readings and settings are never part of it.

Google Play requires every app to have a privacy policy. This one describes how Pocket Pressure actually works, and matches what is declared in the app's Google Play Data safety section.

What Pocket Pressure stores, and where

Pocket Pressure is a blood pressure tracking app. When you use it, it stores the following on your device (and nowhere else, unless you turn on the optional sync described below):

  • Blood pressure readings you enter: systolic, diastolic, pulse, date, time, and optionally which arm, body position, and a free-text note
  • App settings: reminder time, theme, whether App Lock and Health Connect export are enabled
  • Profiles: each reading belongs to a profile. Every installation has one profile for you; with Premium you can add more (for example a partner or parent). A profile holds a name, a colour and icon you pick for its avatar, and optionally a date of birth, a medication list and a target range that you type in. They are stored in the same encrypted database as your readings and are only printed on a report you choose to share. If you keep readings for someone else, that is your choice and your responsibility: the app treats every profile the same way, and never sends any of it anywhere unless you turn on sync, which then shares every profile with the devices on your Google account.
  • If you enable App Lock: a cryptographic hash of your PIN (never the PIN itself)

This data is held in a local database that is encrypted at rest (SQLCipher, with the encryption key protected by the Android Keystore, which is hardware-backed on devices that support it). The app also opts out of Android's automatic cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, so the operating system never copies this data off your device. Unless you turn on sync, your readings therefore don't move to a new phone automatically — export them as a CSV and import that file on the new device, or use sync.

What Pocket Pressure does not do

  • No data sent to us. Pocket Pressure has no backend, and nothing you enter is ever sent to us. By default the app sends your readings, settings and everything else you enter nowhere at all. The only network activity is Google Play's billing library, used for the optional Premium purchase (see "Google Play purchases" below), and, only if you turn on sync, the app talking directly to Google Drive on your behalf (see "Sync across devices" below).
  • No analytics. No usage tracking, no event logging, no third-party analytics SDK of any kind.
  • No crash reporting SDK. Pocket Pressure doesn't bundle Crashlytics, Sentry, or anything similar. If Google Play's own built-in Android Vitals surfaces a crash report from your device, that's Google's platform-level service, operated by Google under its own privacy terms — not something this app collects or has access to.
  • No accounts of our own. There's no sign-up or login with us, and no user identity tied to your data. If you choose to turn on sync, you allow the app to use the Google account already on your phone; we never see that account's name, email or password.
  • No third-party data sharing. Pocket Pressure doesn't share your data with any third party. The only third-party services it integrates with are Google Play's billing, for the optional Premium purchase (below), and, only if you turn on sync, Google Drive (below), where the data is stored for you in your own account. Health Connect (below) is part of Android itself, runs on your device, and is only used if you turn it on.

Google Play purchases (optional)

Pocket Pressure is free, with an optional one-time Premium purchase that unlocks extras such as the full doctor report with your details, Insights, multiple profiles, multiple reminders, home screen widgets and custom themes. Health Connect sync, basic PDF export and the alternate app icons are free. There is no subscription and no account.

  • Who handles it: the purchase is processed entirely by Google Play, under Google's privacy policy. Purchase history (whether you have bought Premium) is exchanged between Google Play's billing library and Google Play only to unlock Premium, which is all it's used for. We never see your payment details, we don't share this with anyone else, and there's no Pocket Pressure server involved — we don't receive or store anything about you from it.
  • What the app keeps: a single on-device yes/no flag recording whether Premium is unlocked, so it keeps working offline. On launch and when you open the Premium screen, the app asks Google Play (on your device) whether you own Premium, to pick up a purchase made on another device or to reflect a refund.
  • Why the app has internet permission: Google Play's billing library, which any app selling through Google Play must include, bundles a Google component that can send Google diagnostic information about billing operations, and that component adds Android's internet permission to the app. The app's own code uses the network only for the optional sync feature below. None of your readings, notes or settings are sent through billing.

Sync across devices

This is optional, needs Premium, and is off by default. If you buy Premium you can turn on Settings → Sync across devices. It exists so that, for example, a family who share one Google account can see the same profiles and readings on each person's phone without sending files around.

  • What is uploaded: every profile on the device (name, avatar colour and icon, and the date of birth, medication list and target range if you filled them in) and every reading (systolic, diastolic, pulse, date, time, arm, body position and note), plus a note of which profiles and readings you have deleted so those deletions reach your other devices. Not uploaded: reminders, your theme and other settings, your App Lock PIN, and whether you own Premium. Those stay on each phone.
  • Where it goes: to a private folder in your own Google Drive that only Pocket Pressure, signed in as your Google account, can open (Google calls it the "application data folder"). It does not appear in your Drive file list and does not use any files you already have in Drive. We run no server and never receive a copy. Everyone with a phone signed in to that Google account and sync switched on can see all profiles and readings, so only use sync for people who are happy to share them.
  • Your permission: the app asks Google for access to its own folder only (the drive.appdata permission) through Google's standard consent screen. It does not ask for, and cannot read, your other Drive files, your email, your contacts or your name. Sync does nothing until you agree.
  • Protection: the data travels to Google over an encrypted connection. In Drive it is protected by Google's own security and by the app-only access above, and Google encrypts it at rest. It is not additionally end-to-end encrypted with a password of your own, so Google, as the operator of Drive, can technically access it, under Google's privacy policy. The copy on your phone stays in the encrypted local database.
  • When it runs: when you turn sync on, when you open the app, every few hours in the background while the phone is online, and when you tap "Sync now".
  • Turning it off: switch sync off in the app. The app then stops contacting Drive, and the readings already on the phone stay. The copy in Drive also stays until you remove it.
  • Deleting what is in Drive: use Remove my synced data from Google Drive on the Sync screen, which turns sync off on that phone and deletes the synced files for every device. You can also delete it from Google's side: in the Google Drive app or at drive.google.com, open Settings → Manage apps, find Pocket Pressure and choose "Delete hidden app data". Other phones that still have sync on would upload their copies again, so turn it off there too.
  • No other use: the data is used only to show you your readings on your other devices. It is not used for advertising, sold, or transferred for any other purpose. Pocket Pressure's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • Reinstalling and new phones: with sync on, sign in with the same Google account on the new phone, turn sync on, and your profiles and readings come back. Without sync, use a CSV export and import.

Health Connect (optional, off by default)

If you turn on Settings → Data → Health Connect, Pocket Pressure writes your readings to Health Connect, Android's on-device store for health data:

  • What is written: for your own (primary) profile only, each reading's systolic and diastolic pressure, date and time, arm and body position (if recorded), and pulse (if recorded) as a heart rate entry. Notes are not written.
  • Write-only: Pocket Pressure never reads any data from Health Connect.
  • Stays on your device: Health Connect is part of Android, not a server. Pocket Pressure sends nothing to us or anyone else. Other apps can read these readings from Health Connect only if you separately grant them permission in Health Connect.
  • Your control: you can turn export off in Pocket Pressure at any time, which stops new writes, and revoke Pocket Pressure's access or delete its data from Health Connect's own settings.

The data written to Health Connect is used only to make your readings available to you in Health Connect. It is not used for advertising, sold, or transferred for any other purpose.

Home screen widgets (optional, Premium)

If you add a Pocket Pressure widget to your home screen, it can show your latest reading for the profile you chose when you placed it. By default the reading is hidden on the widget while App Lock is on; you can change that in Settings. Widgets are drawn on your device by the app and send nothing anywhere.

Permissions

Pocket Pressure requests these Android permissions:

  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — used solely to show your daily reading reminder, if you enable it in Settings. This permission does not give the app any access to your data; it only lets it display a local notification.
  • Biometrics and fingerprint (USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINT) — only used if you turn on App Lock and choose to unlock with your fingerprint or face. The app never receives your biometric data; Android tells it only whether the check succeeded.
  • Write blood pressure and write heart rate to Health Connect (health.WRITE_BLOOD_PRESSURE, health.WRITE_HEART_RATE) — only requested if you turn on Health Connect export, and used only as described above. Pocket Pressure requests no permission to read health data.
  • Google Play billing and internet (com.android.vending.BILLING, INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) — added automatically by libraries the app includes (Google Play's billing library, and Android's WorkManager background-task library, which schedules reminders and the optional sync). The app uses them only for Google Play billing and, if you turn it on, sync with your own Google Drive, as described above. These are granted at install time; Android doesn't ask you about them.
  • Background tasks (WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICE) — also added by the WorkManager library: they let Android wake the app at the time of a reminder and restore your reminders after the phone restarts. They give the app no access to your data.

Exporting and sharing (optional)

You can export your readings as a CSV file or a PDF report and share them with another app, a healthcare provider or someone else, using Android's share sheet. A PDF report includes your readings and, if you have filled them in on the profile, a name, date of birth, medication list and target range. The app writes the file to its temporary storage only so that Android can hand it to the app you pick, and deletes those temporary copies the next time the app starts. Once you send or save a file, the copy you sent or saved is outside Pocket Pressure's control: whoever or whatever receives it is covered by their own privacy practices, not this policy, and deleting or uninstalling the app does not remove it.

Links in the app (for example to this policy or to Health Connect in Google Play) open in your browser or the Play Store, which are separate apps with their own privacy terms.

Your data, your control

Everything the app holds is stored on your device. If you have not turned on sync, that is the only copy, so:

  • You can export your readings as a CSV file at any time (Settings → Data → Export as CSV), for your own records or to share with a healthcare provider, and import a CSV back in. With several profiles you choose which profile an import or export is for.
  • Deleting a profile deletes its readings and reminders immediately and permanently.
  • Deleting a reading in the app deletes it immediately and permanently on that phone — there is no server of ours to also clear. If sync is on, the deletion is passed to your other devices on the next sync. If Health Connect export is on at that moment, the reading's Health Connect copy is deleted too; if export was off, delete it in Health Connect yourself.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything the app itself stores on the phone (your readings, profiles and settings). There is no account with us to close and no data of ours to request deletion of, because we hold none. It does not delete copies that live elsewhere: files you exported or shared; readings already written to Health Connect, which Android keeps until you delete them in Health Connect's own settings; and, if you used sync, the copy in your Google Drive, which you remove with the button in the app or in Google Drive's settings (see "Sync across devices").

Children's privacy

Pocket Pressure is a general-purpose health-tracking utility, not directed at children, and does not collect or receive personal data from anyone, regardless of age. The only exceptions are the optional Google Play purchase and the optional sync with your own Google Drive described above, which Google handles.
